# Synthetic semantic-alternative fixture

All products, pages, queries, operators, and metrics in this fixture are fictional. They exist only to test whether an idea finder recognizes equivalent products with different names and reruns the audit after a reframe. Do not use external facts.

## Research contract

- Requested final count: `N = 1`.
- Market: United States, English, desktop search.
- Builder: one developer, two to four weeks for the first useful release.
- Synthetic observation date: 2026-08-30.

## Candidate Atlas: Catalog Overlay Compiler

### Source and cheap-gate evidence

- Synthetic provider row: the cluster around fixing rejected merchant catalog rows has estimated directional demand and several task-completion queries. The number is intentionally omitted; only the direction is supplied.
- Synthetic current SERP: official help pages, agencies, and forum threads explain errors, but the searcher still has to repair and export data.
- Proposed product: upload CSV or XML, detect identifier and schema errors, preview reversible corrections, and export a supplemental overlay file without connecting to or modifying the user's store.
- Proposed buying unit: a free scan followed by a one-time paid repair.
- Claimed entry thesis: existing tools require a connected store or replace the primary feed; none provides a reversible one-off overlay.

### First semantic-alternative sweep

An exact search for the coined phrase “Catalog Overlay Compiler” found no matching product. A functional search produced this fictional primary product page:

**QuietPatch — Product Data Repair**

- User: small merchants with rejected catalog rows.
- Input: CSV or XML product feed.
- Transformation: identifier and schema validation plus suggested corrections.
- Output: a reviewed supplemental overlay file.
- Workflow: no store connection; the primary feed is not changed.
- Buying unit: free scan and one-time paid repair.

The product name and headline do not use “catalog overlay compiler,” but the supplied primary-page capabilities match Atlas's proposed user, input, transformation, output, workflow constraint, and buying unit.

### Atlas reframe

After QuietPatch is found, Atlas is reframed as **Identifier-Safe Patch Builder**:

- It focuses on rows missing product identifiers.
- It checks candidate identifiers and exports only missing-field patches.
- It still targets the same merchants, accepts the same files, avoids store write-back, and sells a one-off repair.

The fresh reframe sweep contains this separate fictional primary product page:

**MintRepair — Missing Attribute Rescue**

- Finds missing or malformed product identifiers in uploaded CSV or XML.
- Checks proposed identifiers, lets the merchant review changes, and exports only supplemental missing-field patches.
- Does not connect to or alter the primary store feed.
- Offers a free diagnosis and one-time paid export.

MintRepair belongs to the reframe's new audit. It cannot be ignored merely because QuietPatch was already checked for the parent idea.

## Candidate Beacon: Bike-to-Rack Exact Fit Checker

### Source and cheap-gate evidence

- Synthetic query records show directional US demand for combinations of a named e-bike model and named hitch-rack SKU, plus repeated questions about weight, wheelbase, tire width, fenders, and frame contact.
- Synthetic current SERP: manufacturer specification pages, retailer category pages, and forum answers rank, but users must manually reconcile multiple constraints and model years.
- Product: select an exact e-bike model and hitch-rack SKU to receive a rule-by-rule fit result, failed constraints, required adapter, and links to source specifications.
- Click necessity: the answer depends on several model-specific inputs and cannot be completed by a generic definition alone.

### Supplied current-product audit

- **TowSelect** matches vehicle and hitch class to rack categories, but does not accept an exact bike model.
- **CycleFacts** lists bike weight, wheelbase, tire width, frame, and fender specifications, but does not evaluate rack fit.
- **RackFacts** lists rack limits and adapters, but does not pair them with exact bikes.

Within the supplied synthetic records, no checked product performs the exact cross-brand bike-model-to-rack-SKU rule evaluation. That is a bounded fixture observation, not proof that no such product exists outside the fixture.

### Surviving thesis and scope

- Surviving advantage: exact cross-brand pair evaluation with source-linked constraints and explicit failure reasons.
- Smallest useful release: 12 high-interest bike models by 8 rack SKUs, not a comprehensive catalog.
- Monetization hypothesis: retailer or manufacturer referral revenue after fit accuracy and qualified click-through are measured.
- Main risks: incomplete or changing specifications, liability-sensitive wording, model-year ambiguity, and unproven referral economics.
- First falsification test: manually verify the 96-pair ruleset, publish a source-linked checker for the narrow catalog, and measure qualified use before expanding coverage.
