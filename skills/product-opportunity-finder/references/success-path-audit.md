# Success-path audit

Use this guide when the user supplies a successful project, asks how a product succeeded, or wants opportunities derived from success cases. The purpose is to learn the path and reusable mechanism—not to reproduce the product, interface, output, architecture, or brand.

## Reconstruct the path

Build a dated sequence using primary sources where possible:

1. the initial user problem and how it was observed;
2. the alternatives available at that time;
3. the dissatisfaction or gap that remained;
4. the initial user, trigger, and narrow entry wedge;
5. the product mechanism or differentiator;
6. the first-user and distribution channel;
7. product changes, launches, integrations, or community moments;
8. observed adoption, usage, retention, revenue, or other outcome evidence;
9. later expansion, repositioning, or operating burden;
10. current alternatives and whether the original gap still exists.

Do not collapse stars, downloads, likes, launch votes, website traffic, customers, active usage, retention, and revenue into “traction.” Name the observed measure and its source. Founder statements remain `Claimed` unless independently corroborated.

## Separate product value from amplification

Analyze two systems:

- **Product engine:** the job completed, time or risk saved, repeat trigger, switching reason, data or integration advantage, and retention mechanism.
- **Amplification engine:** launches, social sharing, open source, templates, community status, platform distribution, SEO, partnerships, or a founder audience.

An amplification event can explain attention without proving durable product value. Product usefulness can also exist without making the original distribution path reproducible for a new entrant.

## Judge what is reusable

Classify each success factor:

- **Replicable:** a method another builder can execute, such as observing issues, entering through one workflow, or distributing inside an existing community.
- **Conditionally replicable:** requires a particular audience, integration, trust position, dataset, domain expertise, or budget.
- **Timing-dependent:** relies on a platform launch, regulatory change, cost curve, or temporary attention window.
- **Resource-dependent:** relies on a team, capital, proprietary data, partnerships, or an installed user base.
- **Luck or unknown:** the evidence does not establish a controllable cause.

Do not convert correlation into causation. If the timeline shows a social spike followed by repository growth, describe the sequence; do not claim the post caused durable adoption without supporting data.

## Derive adjacent opportunities

Extract transferable problem and distribution patterns, then generate candidates through material changes such as:

- same job for a different reachable segment with distinct constraints;
- adjacent step before or after the successful product's workflow;
- same trigger with a different input, output, integration, trust level, or buying unit;
- a missing collaboration, governance, audit, migration, support, or compliance layer;
- a workflow newly enabled by the same technical or platform change;
- a higher-service or lower-friction model where the current economics leave users under-served.

Each derived candidate must return to the normal source-record, legibility, functional-alternative, execution, and distribution gates. Similarity to a successful product is not validation. A material reframe requires a fresh functional fingerprint and current alternatives audit.

## Report the lesson

Keep the success-case section separate from the opportunity recommendation:

1. **What happened:** dated facts and bounded metrics.
2. **Why it appears to have worked:** explicit inference with competing explanations.
3. **What is reusable:** mechanisms within the user's reach.
4. **What is not safely transferable:** timing, audience, data, capital, brand, or unknown causes.
5. **What opportunity this suggests:** a newly audited product thesis, not a clone.

If the available sources support only a partial timeline, say which causal link remains unknown. Do not fill the gap with a polished founder myth.
