# Full opportunity report template

Use this template only for an end-to-end study. Keep the executive call short; put traceability in the later sections.

## 1. Executive call

- **Decision:** build now, run a bounded test, watch, or do not build.
- **First choice:** one opportunity and one-sentence reason.
- **Backup:** one opportunity and why it is second.
- **Confidence:** high, medium, or low, followed by the decisive evidence gap.

## 2. Research frame

State target country, language, device if relevant, builder constraints, objective, evidence cutoff, excluded categories, and assumptions.

## 3. Candidate comparison

| Candidate | User job | Demand | SERP | Product value | Solo fit | Economics | Confidence | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use `strong`, `mixed`, `weak`, or `unknown` in evidence columns. Put source links and metric details below the table rather than cramming them into cells.

## 4. First choice

Cover:

- Exact user and job.
- Entry query or cluster without adding overlapping volumes.
- Current result landscape and consolidated operators.
- Why the proposed product fulfills the intent better.
- First indexable page or functional product.
- Two-to-four-week implementation boundary.
- Distribution and monetization path.
- Evidence that supports the call, with source, market, and date.
- Strongest counterargument.

## 5. Backup

Explain what makes it credible, why it ranks second, and the condition that would promote it to first.

## 6. Rejected candidates

List material candidates and the decisive reason for rejection. Existing competitors alone are not a rejection reason.

## 7. Entry plan

Specify:

1. The smallest useful product or page.
2. The first acquisition experiment.
3. The measurement event and data source.
4. The time and cash budget.
5. What not to build yet.

## 8. Risk register

Separate ranking risk, click/AI risk, product risk, operational risk, legal or licensing risk, and monetization risk. Distinguish known risk from unknown evidence.

## 9. Stop line

Use a dated, measurable condition, for example: “Stop expanding after six weeks if the working product is indexed, has demonstrated page-one or otherwise meaningful visibility, yet has fewer than N non-branded Search Console impressions across the validated cluster and no improving week-over-week trend.” Choose thresholds from the opportunity's baseline; do not copy arbitrary numbers. Search Console impressions are exposure conditioned on indexation and rank, so low impressions before visibility do not prove low market demand.

## 10. Sources and evidence limits

Group sources as primary/official, first-party measurement, specialist estimate, community/owner claim, and inference. Note inaccessible sources, stale snapshots, locale limits, and anything not independently verified.
