# Opportunity report template

Use this template for a completed end-to-end idea study. The default answer should help the user understand the products and choose an action; it should not read like an internal research log.

## 1. Result first

Open in the user's language with:

- how many qualifying ideas were found;
- which one ranks first;
- whether the first choice is suitable for a bounded build/test or a smaller acquisition experiment; and
- the most important remaining risk.

Example:

```text
我找到了 3 个通过基础门槛的方向。首选是……，因为……。它适合先做一个可用的小版本验证，最大风险是……。
```

Do not open with the research mode, candidate-pool mechanics, evidence labels, tool trace, or an English action taxonomy.

If fewer than `N` candidates passed because of a genuine hard limit or access blocker, do not use the completed-study opening. Say plainly:

```text
这轮只确认了 M/N 个合格方向，任务还没有完成；缺少的是……。
```

Rejected and unknown-only ideas do not fill the missing slots.

## 2. Rank exactly the qualifying final ideas

For each final idea, use a concrete product name or plain product description as the heading. Include:

1. **Product:** what the user would actually build.
2. **User and job:** who searches, in what situation, and what must be completed.
3. **Search entry:** the exact query or coherent intent cluster.
4. **Origin:** the observed site, page, query, customer record, or dated change that surfaced the idea.
5. **Demand:** the strongest directional demand or ranking-page traffic-potential evidence, with source, market, date, metric definition, and limitation.
6. **SERP and alternatives:** what currently ranks, whether operators are consolidated, what result features remove clicks, which functional product fingerprint was checked, and which closest primary product surfaces were opened. Phrase non-observation as bounded by the checked sources and date.
7. **Surviving edge:** the material product, segment, data, workflow, distribution, trust, service, speed, or economics advantage that remains.
8. **Why a click is necessary:** the interaction, calculation, comparison, upload, configuration, monitoring, play, transaction, or proprietary information the search result cannot finish.
9. **Execution:** smallest useful product, first indexable page, monetization or strategic value, distribution requirement, and recurring work.
10. **Action:** what to do now, the main risk, and the first test that could disprove the thesis.

Write the action in plain language, such as `建议先做一个可用的小版本验证` or `先验证获客再开发完整产品`. Do not make the user decode `Research lead`, `Action ceiling`, or similar internal labels.

## 3. Explain the first choice

After listing all final ideas, state why #1 outranks the others:

- stronger or more persistent user job;
- better topic-level traffic potential rather than merely a larger exact keyword;
- clearer click and product necessity;
- more attainable current SERP and distribution requirement;
- stronger business potential;
- more defensible entry thesis; and
- better fit for the user's time, money, skills, and maintenance capacity.

Name the strongest counterargument. Do not hide it inside a generic risk list.

## 4. Use a comparison table only when it helps

For more than two ideas, a compact table can clarify the decision:

| Rank | Product | User job | Demand | Search access | Surviving edge | Solo fit | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use `strong`, `mixed`, `weak`, or `unknown` only when the terms are explained by the surrounding evidence. Do not convert them into a precise composite score.

## 5. Give the first build or acquisition test

Specify:

1. the smallest genuinely useful product or page;
2. the acquisition path being tested;
3. the qualified exposure or user group;
4. the behavior that indicates the job was completed;
5. the cost and recurring work to measure; and
6. what not to build yet.

If search access is the main uncertainty, test visibility or acquisition before interpreting product conversion. If the product itself is uncertain, use a working product rather than a fake tool or waitlist.

Every numerical stopping threshold needs a basis: a user target, observed baseline, sample-size goal, experiment cost and minimum acceptable return, or a calibration period and formula. Do not invent a precise four-week, impression, conversion, or revenue threshold merely to make the report look decisive.

## 6. Show only decisive evidence by default

Include four to eight decisive rows when a table improves trust:

| Claim | Source or record | Evidence class | Market and date | What it supports | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Keep keyword volume, ranking-page traffic potential, site traffic, impressions, clicks, difficulty, links, and revenue separate. Mark owner or community stories as claims. State when a product page was opened versus seen only in a search result.

## 7. Add audit mechanics only on request

When the user asks for diligence, reproducibility, a saved artifact, or a research audit, append:

- selected discovery mode and primary data spine;
- source-record ledger and rejected/reframed candidates;
- query and intent-cluster mapping;
- provider definitions, observation dates, and data periods;
- semantic current-product parity checks, including the functional fingerprint, checked coverage, bounded non-observations, and fresh audits for material reframes;
- searches, opened pages, and tested workflows reconciled to the tool trace;
- user-supplied workload limit and why collection stopped; and
- authorized artifact paths.

Do not include this appendix in an ordinary “find me an idea” answer. Research transparency matters, but it should not bury the product recommendation.
