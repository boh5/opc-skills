# Semantic current-alternative audit

Use this audit for every candidate competing for a final slot. Its purpose is to find products that perform the same job under different names before expensive validation is spent defending a false gap.

Competitor existence is not a veto. The audit tests whether the proposed entry thesis still exists.

## 1. Build the functional fingerprint

Record the candidate without relying on its proposed name:

- target user and triggering situation;
- input, source format, or connected ecosystem;
- transformation, decision, or workflow performed;
- output or result the user receives;
- important constraints such as no login, no write-back, reversibility, privacy, locality, or speed;
- buying unit and monetization shape, such as one-off repair, subscription, lead, transaction, or free utility.

Two products may be near-clones even when their names and headline keywords do not overlap. Compare the job, input, transformation, output, and workflow before comparing labels.

## 2. Search by function and market language

Cover the applicable discovery surfaces rather than repeating one coined phrase:

- the user's exact query and task language;
- input-to-output combinations and workflow verbs;
- synonyms for the action, artifact, error, or desired result;
- the ecosystem's marketplace, app directory, integration category, or product taxonomy;
- current alternatives, comparisons, category leaders, and adjacent tools used for the same job;
- adjacent geography or language only when the product can travel across markets or the other market helps reveal the product category.

There is no universal query count. Stop early when primary evidence establishes decisive parity. Otherwise continue until the material fingerprint dimensions have been covered well enough to make a bounded, dated claim. Search snippets and directory cards are leads, not observed product behavior.

## 3. Verify on primary product surfaces

For each plausible alternative, open the current official landing page, documentation, pricing page, app listing, or usable workflow needed to verify the decisive claim. Record:

- product and primary URL;
- observation date and market or availability boundary;
- matched fingerprint elements;
- whether parity is full, partial, absent, or unresolved;
- the claimed entry advantage that survives or fails;
- any limitation, stale page, unavailable workflow, or ambiguous wording.

A plausible near-clone that cannot be resolved from a primary surface remains a decisive unknown. It blocks final admission; it does not justify calling the candidate unique.

## 4. Decide what the evidence invalidates

- **Full or decisive parity:** invalidate the current entry thesis. Reject the candidate as framed unless another material, source-supported advantage remains.
- **Partial parity:** keep only the specific segment, data, workflow, distribution, trust, service, speed, quality, or economics advantage that the evidence still supports.
- **Competitor-filled but attractive market:** it may remain viable when demand is meaningful and a material entry thesis survives. Do not reject it merely for having competitors.
- **No observed equivalent:** say “no equivalent was observed in the checked sources as of DATE,” name the coverage boundary internally, and keep residual uncertainty. Do not say that no such product exists.

“Better,” “simpler,” “AI-powered,” or “cheaper” is not a surviving advantage without a concrete product difference and evidence that it matters to the target user.

## 5. Re-audit every material reframe

A reframe is material when it changes the user, query, input, output, workflow, data, distribution, buying unit, or economics. For each material reframe:

1. Write a new functional fingerprint.
2. Generate new search language from the changed elements.
3. Check the relevant category and primary product surfaces again.
4. Admit the reframe only if a supported advantage survives the new audit.

Do not inherit an “alternatives checked” result from the parent idea. A narrower label on the same underlying product is not a reframe.

## 6. Converge without lowering the bar

Once parity or a hard blocker decisively kills the sole entry thesis, log the reason and replenish from the next source-backed record. Do not complete a full demand, SERP, economics, and launch report for a candidate that can no longer enter the final set.

Deep-check surviving candidates only. Stop normal collection when the requested `N` candidates have enough decisive evidence to pass; continue beyond `N` only when the user asks for a broader landscape or comparison set.
